1. Purpose and scope

Rishivan answers questions about a birth chart using classical Jyotish texts, and grounds every statement it makes in a specific verse rather than in model recall.

One constraint shapes every decision in this document:

Design invariant

A claim that cannot be traced to a source verse must not be made. Not "should be cited where possible" — a claim without a resolvable citation is withheld, and the system says so rather than paraphrasing plausibly.

This document covers the system as it currently exists: the offline pipeline that turns scanned books into executable rules, and the request-time pipeline that turns a chart and a question into a cited answer. It is descriptive, not aspirational — where something is incomplete or unresolved, Section 10 says so plainly rather than presenting an intended end state as the current one.

Out of scope: UI implementation detail, ephemeris and chart mathematics (treated here as a trusted dependency), deployment and infrastructure.

2. System overview — two pipelines, one artefact

The system is two pipelines that meet at a single artefact: a directory of compiled YAML rules. Keeping them separate is deliberate, and it is the reason serving is cheap and predictable.

Build-time (offline)Serve-time (per question)
JobBooks → executable rulesChart + question → cited answer
CostLLM spend; once per bookFixed; no extraction, ever
ReadsPostgres, JSONL on diskYAML on disk, Qdrant
TriggerOperator runs a CLIUser asks a question
Worst failureA wrong rule reaches diskA wrong answer, or a needless refusal

Rules are compiled artefacts. Nothing at request time calls a model to interpret a verse; that work is already done, checked, and frozen. The practical consequence is that answer latency and answer cost are independent of corpus size, and that a bad rule is a build-time defect we can find with tests rather than a runtime surprise.

4.1 Bridge and triage

Bridging pairs each Sanskrit shloka with its English translation and derives the locator — chapter, verse_ref_local, page_from, page_to. Each book carries an EditionProfile: data, not code, describing how that particular edition prints its chapter headings.

Triage then routes each unit to a destination — rule (states a rule), item (a definition, destined for the knowledge base), or ambiguous. Both steps are deterministic and free, and triage is the cost gate: it decides which units are worth spending model calls on.

Five books are refused, by design

BookWhy the bridge refuses it
vivaha-patalamEntirely Devanagari; no Latin headings, no numbered divisions. 383 shlokas, zero English prose elements.
laghu-parashariSections are named, not numbered.
dharma-sindhuNo chapter headings survive in the OCR at all.
devakeralam vol 1 & 2Prints "BOOK I (Containing 2718 Slokas)" — no chapter structure.

Filing these verses under an invented chapter number would fabricate the locator for an entire book, so the bridge skips rather than guesses. The cost is real and worth naming: vivaha-patalam is the marriage-timing text, and therefore the single most relevant source for the marriage question that originally exposed thin coverage.

The four numerology books are excluded for a different reason. The rule vocabulary is planet/sign/house-shaped and has no representation for a mulank; across all four books triage found 12 rule-bearing units. Extracting them would be spend with nowhere to land.

4.2 Extraction — and why it is now one call

Two modes exist and share all validation and compilation downstream.

Six-call mode (original): classify → extract ×2 → reconcile → verify → back-translate. Double extraction at differing temperatures surfaces disagreement; verify is adversarial, seeing the source and the JSON but not the extractor's reasoning; back-translate renders the JSON to prose without the verse, so divergence signals lost meaning.

Single-call mode (current): one call, extracting and classifying together. Adopted because review is manual and external — a verifier verdict the human reviewer will overrule is five calls spent pre-empting a judgement that was never the pipeline's to make. An empty rules array is the classification.

The switch was measured on the same 24 passages, not assumed:

ModeModel callsRules produced
Six-call582
Single-call246

Fewer calls and more rules — the reconcile round was discarding rules the single pass keeps.

Everything free and deterministic is retained: verbatim-quote checking, validate_candidate, the full compiler pass sequence, and the review queue ordered by impact × uncertainty. What is lost is recorded, not implied: verification_skipped states why no verifier ran, and the generated YAML header repeats it, so a rule nothing examined can never be mistaken for one that passed a verifier.

4.3 The closed vocabulary

The model may only emit symbols the registry defines. This is the mechanism that makes extraction checkable rather than merely plausible.

CountKindExamples
34predicatesoccupies_bhava(subject, bhava), varga_occupies(varga, subject, rashi), dasha_active(system, subject, level)
25claimsrelationship.harmony, wealth.accumulation, career.disruption
12domainscareer, education, health, longevity, progeny, property, relationship, spiritual, status, temperament, travel, wealth
6schoolsparashari, jaimini, prashna, lalkitab, tajika, muhurta
153entity symbolsgraha.*, rashi.*, bhava.*, dignity.*, band.*, dist.*, ref.*, varga.*, nakshatra.*

Three things are forbidden — approximating into the nearest predicate, dropping content, and inventing a symbol. One thing is required: where the vocabulary falls short the model must emit an ExtensionProposal. That is a correct and expected outcome, not a failure. The corpus is supposed to tell us what the vocabulary is missing.

Why schools are namespaced, not merged

The traditions genuinely disagree. Jaimini uses rashi drishti where Parashari uses graha drishti — the same chart yields different aspects. Lal Kitab remedies contradict Parashari outright. Muhurta was given its own school this cycle for the same reason: a muhurta rule reasons about an elected moment, and filing it under Parashari would let it fire on a birth chart it was never about.

4.4 Identity is stamped by the caller, never authored by the model

Rule IDs and provenance are derived from the citation:

{BOOK}.{TOPIC}.{LOCATOR}.X{nnnn}      extracted   e.g. HINDUPREDICTIVE.LONGEVITY.CH14V5.X0001
{BOOK}.{TOPIC}.{LOCATOR}.{nnnn}       converted   (the X makes collision structurally impossible)

This was learned expensively, and it is worth the architect knowing both incidents because they characterise the failure class:

  • The engine stopped starting. A model mistyped a verse number into an ID, two different Phaladeepika rules shared PD.28.63.0002, and the compiler refused the whole bundle — correctly, since a duplicate ID fires twice and counts as two independent sources under noisy-OR. One wrong digit in 274 rules took the application down.
  • One book appeared as three. source.book used setdefault, so the model's guess won: phaladeepika, Phaladeepika and phaladeepika-sastri-1950 all appeared. This one fails silently — an unrecognised book scores neutral in the source-authority matrix and drops out of weighting without ever raising.

Both are now overwritten from the passage, with mutation-verified regression tests — reverting to setdefault fails four of the five.

4.5 Gate and compile

Candidate documents pass through the compiler: symbol resolution, typecheck, closure, DNF expansion, core extraction, contradiction and realizability checks, duplicate-ID and claim-polarity passes. Failures are dropped with their diagnostic attached; only survivors reach disk, and every one lands as status: candidate.

A limit worth knowing before reading the numbers

A rule is expanded into every combination its any groups permit, capped at 32 variants. Groups multiply when they sit side by side inside an all. One verse — "the quadrants are occupied by benefics" — expanded to four houses × four benefics = 1,536 variants and was refused. The engine's own diagnostic now names the offending rule, so the gate drops it before it reaches disk instead of the failure surfacing at next startup.

5.1 Rule retrieval is not similarity search

This is the central design decision, and it inverts conventional RAG.

A conventional RAG asks "which rules look like this question?" Koonji asks "which rules are true of this chart?" — exact boolean evaluation against interned chart atoms. Similarity enters only afterwards, to rank which of the true rules to display.

Measured, not assumed

Nominating by similarity first lost 11 to 14 of the 21 rules genuinely true of a test chart. A similarity window cannot prefer what it has no way of knowing is true. Recall is therefore total by construction, and the failure mode conventional RAG has here — the rule is in the corpus, the embedding does not match, it never fires, and nobody finds out because you cannot compute recall against an unknown denominator — is eliminated rather than mitigated.

Mechanically, inside Engine.read():

  1. all_chart_tokens(chart, when=query_time) produces interned atoms. Dated by the reading, not the wall clock — matching dasha tokens against today while every other token came from a stated moment would evaluate a Prashna cast for the wrong time.
  2. A retrieval_plan narrows candidates by status, school and domain.
  3. The RuleIndex matches by set containment over an inverted index (atom → variants requiring it). There is not a single vector reference in engine.py or index.py.
  4. Surviving rules fire, honouring exceptions.
  5. Results enter an evidence graph: noisy-OR combination, plus restatement clustering so three paraphrases of one verse count as one piece of evidence rather than three.

The no-false-negatives invariant is maintained by excluding from the index core anything set membership cannot decide — negated leaves, numeric comparisons, unbound variables, and the undecidable. Each exclusion can only widen the candidate set; the VM then prunes with exact arithmetic. False positives are free; a false negative would be invisible forever.

5.2 Passage retrieval — the other half, still Qdrant

Runs alongside rule matching and answers a different question: which pages should the user be shown. When a chart exists, collect_chart_context deliberately does not search the question. It embeds each of roughly 30 chart facts as its own query, ranks pages by how many distinct facts landed on them × source authority × the Book×Rishi matrix, takes the top 20, and widens each by ±1 page so a shloka spanning a page break arrives intact. These become the "DRAWN FROM" chips.

Division of labour

Pages come from Qdrant. Rules come from Koonji. Conflating the two is what produced a screen that could truthfully report "20 sources, 10 classical rules" while simultaneously refusing to answer — see §6.

5.3 Answer gating

build_answer_plan() decides what may be said. A claim must clear a 0.35 confidence floor. The plan is insufficient if nothing cleared it, or if every Rishi abstained. sakshi_node audits the plan before narration, and re_examine exists to send it back rather than let a weak plan through.

6. Architectural change this cycle — one rule system, not two

Before. The rules panel ("N classical rules match this chart") was filled by the older system reading Qdrant, while the Koonji engine ran separately and fed the written answer. The two held rules in incompatible formats and counted different corpora.

The consequence was worse than untidiness. Every rule extracted since the format changed — 274 at the time — fired correctly in the reading while being invisible in the panel. The counts on screen and the refusal underneath them were reporting on different indexes, so both could be true at once and the screen was still misleading.

After. retrieve_node builds the panel from the Koonji reading. koonji_read runs before it in STATIC_EDGES, so nothing is re-evaluated. Two new modules:

  • koonji/panel.py — a reading, in the shape the panel reads.
  • koonji/describe.py — a URF condition rendered in English, so the panel can state why a rule fired (occupies_rashi(graha.moon, rashi.aries) → "the Moon is in Aries").

The switch surfaced three real bugs, which is the argument for having made it: every rule was cited as "BPHS" (a hardcoded string, harmless while BPHS was the only sourced book); the panel came back silently empty because a bare attribute access raised inside the node's catch-all; and 39 rules carried no domain tag, making them uncitable by any Rishi.

8. Model selection, cost and concurrency

Single-call mode uses the deep model only; --fast-model is inert, having served the classify and back-translate stages that single-call skips.

ModelRemaining 2,695 callsNotes
gemini-2.5-pro~$114Output is 91% of the bill

Measured on a real run: 304 calls in 33 seconds at 16 workers. The cost figures derive from measured token counts on completed books, not from list prices applied to estimates.

8.1 Prompt caching — now viable, worth doing later

Explicit context caching on flash-lite has a hard 4,096-token minimum; implicit caching reported no hits at all. The system prompt was 2,641 tokens and did not qualify. After this cycle's prompt work it is 4,509 tokens and now qualifies — reached by adding content that fixed real defects, not by padding to clear a threshold.

Enabling it requires client work (create the cache, pass cached_content, manage TTL) and would take input cost from roughly $0.98 to $0.31 across the remaining corpus. Real, but bounded by the input bill — it cannot compound, and it is not the constraint worth optimising first.

8.2 Concurrency required four thread-safety fixes

Three of these appear only under load, which is why they are listed: they are the class of defect a sequential proving run cannot find.

  • Client lazy-init race. Two threads each built a Vertex client; the second assignment won, the first was garbage-collected and closed its transport mid-request. Symptom: "client has been closed" raised against perfectly good input, seven times in sixteen passages. Fixed with double-checked locking.
  • Budget.spend unlocked calls += 1. The class exists to be a hard ceiling; an unlocked counter undercounts, defeating its only guarantee.
  • Bare JSON array. The contract says "not a bare array" and models return one anyway. The six-call path normalised it in reconcile; single-call lost whole passages until the parser accepted both shapes.
  • Bare KeyError for missing blocks and fields. Now content errors that name the rule, the assertion kind and what was expected.
Correction to prior reporting

Helicone was tagging every extraction call gemini-2.5-flash regardless of which model actually ran, because the dashboard property was stamped from --fast-model while the request used --deep-model. All historical cost attribution in that dashboard is wrong. Fixed. Authoritative sources are the CLI budget: line and GCP billing.

9.1 The fabrication tripwire earns its strictness

The clearest catch: a passage consisting only of the chapter heading "Certain self-evident Rajayogas." produced a complete, confident Raja Yoga rule — ellipses and all — invented entirely from model knowledge. Blocked, because the quote did not appear in the passage.

9.2 Two extraction defects found and fixed this cycle

Both were found by inspecting output against source verses, not by any automated check — and that is the point: both produced rules that passed every automated control.

Dropped narrowing clauses

VERSE    "Even if the Moon is in the 8th no danger of Balarishta is indicated
          PROVIDED she occupies the Drekkanas of Mercury, Jupiter and Venus."

EXTRACTED  all[ occupies_bhava(graha.moon, bhava.08) ]        ← proviso gone

The rule now asserts protection for exactly the charts the verse warns about. This is worse than producing nothing: it is well-formed, correctly cited, quote-verbatim, and passes every check. Only reading the logic against the verse reveals it is backwards.

Invented conditions — the mirror error

VERSE      "Sagittarius. — Mars is the best planet and in conjunction with
            Jupiter produces much good."

EXTRACTED  all[ bhava_in_rashi(bhava.01, rashi.sagittarius),
                occupies_rashi(graha.mars, rashi.sagittarius),   ← invented
                conjunct(graha.mars, graha.jupiter) ]

An over-specified rule is not a cautious rule. It fires on fewer charts than the verse covers, so a reading silently loses evidence it was entitled to — and nothing in the output ever reveals that a rule failed to fire.

Both were fixed by adding a condition-completeness block to the prompt, covering both directions, and by teaching the non-obvious expansions the vocabulary requires (varga_occupies is indexed by rashi, so "the drekkanas of Mercury" must become the D3 signs Mercury owns). Also added: the value families for every argument kind, and the domain list — neither had ever been shown to the model, which is why it substituted bhava.01 into a distance slot and inferred domain.family from the claim family.father.

Verified on held-out verses — not the prompt's own examples. Yield on hindu-predictive went from 47 to 112 rules over the same 304 passages, and the two defect classes did not recur.

10. Risks and decisions requested

10.1 Unreviewed rules are being served — decision needed

Decision 1 · the one I would not ship past

SERVED_STATUSES = {"candidate", "production"}, and every rule in the engine is candidate — including the eight hand-written ones. Nothing is production, so that filter admits everything.

The approval gate lives in Postgres (approved_at IS NOT NULL, enforced by MATCHABLE_PREDICATE) and gates embed_rules.py → Qdrant. Since the §6 panel switch, nothing at serve time reads Postgres. Approving a rule therefore has no effect, and neither does declining to.

To be precise about the history, because it is not a regression introduced carelessly: the written answer was never gated on approval — Koonji has always read YAML directly. The panel was gated, because it came from Qdrant and embed_rules.py publishes only approved rules. The panel switch removed the last place approval mattered.

Recommendation. Move the gate to where the rules live: approval flips YAML status from candidate to production, and serving accepts only production. The Postgres rule table then drops out cleanly and there is one rule system end to end.

The caveat that makes this a decision rather than a task. Flipping that switch today serves 8 rules instead of 1,503, because nothing has ever been marked production. It needs a deliberate first pass — either bulk-promote the 1,109 converted BPHS rules (they came from the older reviewed pipeline), or accept candidate for now and record it as a known, signed-off gap.

10.2 One bad rule can still stop the engine — policy to re-affirm

Decision 2

Engine.from_rules() calls raise_for_errors(), so a single unindexable or duplicate-ID rule prevents startup. This has happened twice — once from a duplicate ID, once from the 1,536-variant explosion.

Both root causes are now addressed (IDs stamped from the citation; index failures named per-rule so the gate drops them before they reach disk). But the underlying policy — refuse to start rather than serve a corpus with a known error — is a deliberate choice, and I would rather it were re-affirmed explicitly than left as an accident of where the check sits.

10.3 The legacy matcher cannot fire current-format rules

satisfies() returns False for an unrecognised atom type rather than raising — inert by design, "not explosive" as the code puts it. Rules loaded into Postgres and embedded to Qdrant therefore sit permanently silent there.

Harmless today, since Koonji feeds the answer. But it means the older system is dead weight rather than a fallback, and a fallback is what its presence implies. Recommendation: retire it deliberately. Maintaining two matchers by default is precisely how the counts diverged in §6.

10.4 Five books remain unreachable

They yield nothing and always will under the verse-unit model. Page-level extraction is the only path. Since citations are internal reference rather than user-facing prose, page granularity is acceptable — and the UI already displays page numbers.

Proposed as a second, explicitly-labelled tier (citation_granularity: page) so a page-cited rule can never be presented as equal in precision to a verse-cited one. Two trade-offs to accept going in: it loses the triage cost gate, since every page contains something; and it risks under-enumeration on dense pages, because a model given a batch tends to return a stable three to five items regardless of the true count.

10.5 Observability gaps

  • Helicone stores empty request and response bodies for the Vertex passthrough. Its own error names the cause — "Body has already been used": the gateway reads the stream to forward it and finds nothing left to log. Token counts and latency are accurate; bodies are not. Use --record to capture exchanges for inspection.
  • Helicone shows $0.00 for flash-lite — no price entry for the model, so it computes zero.

10.6 Smaller items, recorded so they are not rediscovered

  • Roughly 151 rules corpus-wide carry no domain tag, including 100 pre-existing BPHS rules. An untagged rule derives no Rishi affinity and can be cited by nobody — it is in the base and permanently silent.
  • The bridge admits front matter and chapter headings as citable units — about 5% of spend, and prasna-marga is 68% table-of-contents. A deterministic filter is free and removes a fabrication vector, since a heading is exactly the input that makes a model invent what should follow.
  • Classifier fields needs_birth_data and is_followup are computed on every request and read by nothing.
  • The smalltalk decision is an unchecked single point of failure: misclassify a real question and the entire analytic path is skipped, returning a greeting. The deterministic router already scores 11 of 12 on this and could veto.

12. Summary for the reviewer

What I believe is sound

Rule matching is exhaustive by construction rather than best-effort, and that choice was measured against the alternative rather than assumed. Provenance is stamped by the caller and never authored by a model, after two incidents that demonstrated why. Every generated artefact records what did not happen to it, so an unverified rule cannot be mistaken for a verified one. Extraction cost fell 23× with no measured quality loss, and the two defects that did appear were found by held-out testing and fixed.

What needs your decision

The approval gate is disconnected: unreviewed rules reach users, and approval is a no-op in both directions. Everything else in Section 10 can be scheduled. That one I would not ship past, and the reason it is a decision rather than a fix is that closing it naively drops the served rule base from 1,503 to 8.

What is deferred deliberately

The legacy Qdrant matcher, the five unbridgeable books, and prompt caching — each with a recorded rationale rather than an assumption, and each listed above rather than left implicit.
